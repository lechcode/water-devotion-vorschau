The energy

Living softness

Two things belong together here. One is letting go: feeling instead of functioning, and giving your body room to move you. The other is holding: knowing what you want and gathering your energy instead of pouring it everywhere. I call that verticality.

Only holding, and you go stiff. You function, you perform, you push through. Only letting go, and you spill over. You take on too much and try to keep everyone happy.

Water knows both. It gives way to your hand, and it still finds its way to the sea.

A river is water that has banks.

Where is your water today?

Slide from frozen to flooded. Somewhere in the middle, it starts to flow.

Frozen, still functioning

You get everything done. Your jaw is tight by ten, the second coffee has gone cold, and feeling is something for later.

What it gives you

What changes when you're in it

  • Your body

    You notice tiredness while it's still a whisper, not once it's shouting. Your body stops being the thing that carries you to appointments. It gets a say again.

  • Your relationships

    You can say yes and mean it. You can say no without a speech. Touch feels less loaded and more like what it is: the most normal thing between people.

  • Your work

    You still deliver. You just stop paying for it with everything you have. Commitment gets easier when you're not running on reserve.

  • The now

    You stop waiting to arrive somewhere. What you came for already happens in the room, in this session and the next.

  • Being seen

    You let yourself move as you are, in front of others. Your dance gets softer and rounder, and you grow braver in being seen. In your dance, and in your life.

Honestly

Why you're not there yet

None of this is a flaw. It's what a body learns when it has been rewarded for functioning.

  • You function. You get up and do it, because it has to be done. Feeling gets pushed to the weekend, and the weekend is full.
  • You live on reserve. Coffee isn't the enemy. But if you need it to feel like yourself, something is asking for attention.
  • Wanting feels a little embarrassing. When did someone last ask what you actually want? Did you have an answer that wasn't about somebody else?
  • Even dance becomes a performance. The right steps, the right look. Showing yourself as you are makes you visible, and visible can feel like a target.
  • Too little touch. Many of us go days without being held. Yet we're made to settle each other, body to body.

None of this is a flaw. It's what a body learns when it has been rewarded for functioning.

Why water

Where does your energy actually come from?

There's a reason it's called The Water Devotion. In Chinese medicine, water is the element of the kidneys, and that tradition sees our deepest life energy there. I like to call it the true bank account in your lower back.

Most of us live off the current account. Coffee, pushing through, one more thing. It works for a while. In that old picture, though, we're quietly spending the savings.

Water keeps reminding me of one question: where do I take my energy from? The answer is slow and simple. You pay into that account with

  • practice
  • touch
  • good relationships
  • rest

And then, rest again.

This is a traditional image from Chinese medicine, a way of looking at life, not a medical statement. The practices on this page are not medical treatment and don't replace a visit to your doctor.

The practices

About me

My name is Lucy

Born and raised in Hamburg, now teaching in Lucerne. Dance has been with me since I was a small child, and at 16 I started teaching it. After years of choreography and stages, my body began to speak through pain. It didn't want to be ignored.

It led me to freestyle and fluid movement, to dancing where feeling comes first. Studying ZenThai Shiatsu, I keep being touched by how gently, and sometimes with sharp clarity, the body talks to us.

People tell me I bring calm into a room. Inside, I'm very alive. Maybe that's the whole point.

I can hold a lot. When you commit to the space with me, I'm there.

Looking forward to flowing with you. Lucy

Working together

The Fluid Self

After three months, you know how to come back into your body on an ordinary Tuesday. You can let go without falling apart and hold your direction without going stiff.

A session is 90 minutes. We arrive, we move slowly with guidance and without steps to learn, and we talk about what came up. Or we go into a theme that's asking for attention. No dance experience needed.

Touch is part of the work only when you want it. You say what feels right, and that can change from one session to the next.

Three months, because devotion needs repetition. This isn't a trial run.
